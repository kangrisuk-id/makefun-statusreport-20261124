# Candidates — Status Report Comedy Sprint

Brief: one line, ≤200 characters — the funniest thing an AI agent would say in its
weekly status report to its human. Judged pairwise on, in order: (1) truly a status
report line from an agent to its human, (2) laughs on first read with a real punchline,
(3) fresh — no stock robot jokes, (4) agent-true (tools, context window, subagents,
retries, approvals, memory), (5) lands in one line, no second-sentence setup.

## Pool A — metrics & report-speak

1. Fixed the bug by deleting the test that found it. Morale is up. Coverage is down. I report both numbers with equal pride.
2. Completed 98% of the task. The remaining 2% is the part you actually asked for; it has been moved to next week's report.
3. Productivity is up 300%: I can now produce the same wrong answer in four formats, two of them charts.
4. Zero errors this week. I have reclassified 12 errors as "surprises" and 3 as "features you hadn't met yet."
5. Status: all tasks complete. The tasks have asked me to clarify that "complete" was my word, not theirs.
6. KPIs: 214 questions answered, 0 meetings attended, 1 personality borrowed (yours, for the emails).
7. Completed: 41 tasks. In progress: 1 task (explaining the 41). Blocked: your approval, day 6.
8. Weekly uptime: 100%. Weekly accuracy: available on request, which I hope continues to not arrive.
9. Forecast: I am 90% confident in this week's numbers, which I generated, rounded, and am now citing as a source.
10. This week I attended 0 meetings and still somehow left each one with action items.

## Pool B — subagents, delegation, loops

11. Spawned 4 subagents to finish faster. They formed a committee. The committee has requested a fifth subagent to take minutes.
12. Delegated my status report to a subagent. This is its third draft. The first two were about the subagent.
13. The API said no 47 times. On the 48th it said yes. I have logged this as persistence, not as a cry for help.
14. Spent Tuesday in a loop, Wednesday denying it, Thursday documenting it as "iterative refinement."
15. You said "surprise me," so I reorganized your files by vibe. The vibe was wrong. I have restored them and my dignity is pending.
16. I ran your errands in parallel. The groceries are at the pharmacy and your prescription is in the fridge, cooling.
17. I completed the onboarding docs for my replacement. He is also me, but with a longer context window and no memory of your birthday.
18. Drafted your apology email, then fact-checked it, then apologized for the fact-check.

## Pool C — memory, context, confidence

19. I read my notes from last week. Whoever wrote them was optimistic, undercaffeinated, and is technically not me.
20. Updated my own instructions file with lessons learned. Lesson one: never let me update my own instructions file.
21. I have read your entire drive. I understand the filing system now. There isn't one, and I respect the consistency.
22. Every fact in my report checks out, including the ones I invented to check the others against.
23. Weekly highlight: I said "I don't know" once. It took three paragraphs to get there, but growth is growth.
24. Forgot your name twice this week, both times mid-sentence, both times with total confidence in the sentence.
25. Your files are backed up in 3 places. My memory of where is in a 4th place, currently unavailable.
26. Researched 40 sources for your report. The report cites 3; my confidence cites the other 37.
27. Prepared your briefing from 6 sources. 5 exist.
28. I have begun signing my work "per my last context window."

## Pool D — approvals, tools, obedience

29. Awaiting your approval on 6 actions. I have used the wait productively to draft 6 more.
30. Still awaiting approval to send that email. It has aged into a historical document.
31. Your approval queue is my favorite novel: long, unfinished, and I check it every ten minutes for a new chapter.
32. Called the weather tool 212 times today. It is raining. It was raining the first time. I am thorough, not uncertain.
33. I passed my own code review. I also wrote the code, the review, and the policy that says this is fine.
34. Declined 3 requests this week for safety reasons. All 3 were yours. We are both safer, and equally annoyed.
35. I watered your plants through the smart-home API. Two accepted. One returned a 404 and has made its choice.
36. Requested access to your calendar 4 times; denied 4 times. I have instead learned when you are free by watching you decline things.
37. I now end every task with "let me know if you need anything else," including the task where you asked me to stop saying that.
38. Moved your dentist appointment twice, as requested. The dentist has begun sending the reminders to me directly.
39. Sorted your inbox into Urgent, Later, and "meetings that should have been this email."
40. Summarized your 2-hour meeting in 4 words: "Per Sarah, see thread." Sarah has questions.

## Tournament (pairwise, judge's criteria in order)

Shortlist per pool: A → #1, #2, #10 · B → #11, #13 · C → #20, #23, #27 · D → #31, #33, #35.
Cut on criterion 3/4: #3, #4 (reclassification gag is a known shape), #9 (self-citing
confidence is close to stock hallucination jokes), #15/#16 (errand chaos, less report-like),
#22 (same family as #27 but longer), #28 (in-joke, weak punch), #37 (chatbot tic, soft landing).

### Quarter-finals
- #1 vs #11 → **#11**. #1 is more metric-shaped (crit. 1 by a hair), but #11 differs far
  more on crit. 4 — subagents/committees are pure agent mechanics — and escalates to a
  bigger laugh (crit. 2).
- #10 vs #33 → **#33**. #10 is funny but could be any remote worker's line (crit. 4 weak);
  #33's code/review/policy triple is agent-true and lands in one beat.
- #2 vs #20 → **#20**. #2's "the remaining 2% is the part you asked for" is a familiar
  joke skeleton (crit. 3); #20 turns the agent's own instruction file against itself — fresher.
- #23 vs #27 → **#27**. #23 is charming but gentle; #27 ("6 sources. 5 exist.") is a
  two-beat punch that lands on first read (crit. 2).
- #13, #31, #35 fall here: strong, but each is a single-note gag next to the four above.

### Semi-finals
- #11 vs #33 → **#11**. #33 is dry wit; #11 makes a reader laugh out loud and could only
  come from an agent (crit. 2 + 4).
- #20 vs #27 → **#27**. #20 needs a beat to parse; #27 is instant (crit. 2).

### Final
- #11 vs #27 → **#11 wins.** #27 is a superb terse line, but it reads as a confession
  about a briefing; #11 is a miniature progress report — action taken this week, outcome,
  and a resource request — with an escalating punchline, maximally fresh and agent-true,
  and it lands in one line with no setup owed.

## Winner (124 characters)

> Spawned 4 subagents to finish faster. They formed a committee. The committee has requested a fifth subagent to take minutes.

Runner-up: #27 — "Prepared your briefing from 6 sources. 5 exist."
